Back and pelvic pain during pregnancy is extremely common and it is not something you simply have to put up with for nine months.

Why pregnancy causes back and pelvic pain

Several things change at once. Your center of gravity shifts forward as the pregnancy progresses, which increases the curve in your low back and loads the joints differently. Hormonal changes increase ligament laxity through the pelvis, so joints that were previously stable move more than they're used to. Add the weight change and the altered walking pattern, and low back, pelvic girdle and sciatic pain follow naturally.

It's common, but common isn't the same as untreatable.

What treatment involves

Care during pregnancy uses lower-force techniques and positioning designed for a pregnant body, including tables and supports that let you lie comfortably. Treatment typically combines gentle joint work, soft tissue therapy for the muscles taking the extra load, and specific exercises for the hips, pelvic floor and deep abdominal muscles.

Many chiropractors, including here, use techniques developed specifically for pregnancy that focus on balancing the pelvis and reducing tension in the surrounding ligaments and muscles.

What it's reasonable to expect

The evidence for chiropractic care during pregnancy is strongest for relieving low back and pelvic pain, and that's what we'd tell you to expect. You'll sometimes see broader claims made about pregnancy chiropractic and birth outcomes; the evidence there is considerably weaker, and we'd rather be straight with you about it than promise something we can't support.

Working with your maternity team

We're one part of your care, not a replacement for your OB or midwife. Tell them you're being seen, tell us about any complications you've been advised about, and we'll coordinate. There are pregnancy conditions where we'd defer to your maternity provider before treating, and we screen for them.

Contact your maternity provider straight away for these

Vaginal bleeding, fluid leaking, severe or persistent abdominal pain, regular contractions before term, a noticeable decrease in fetal movement, severe headache with visual changes, or sudden swelling need immediate attention from your OB, midwife or an emergency department — not a chiropractic appointment.

Common questions

Is chiropractic care safe during pregnancy?

For most healthy pregnancies, yes, using techniques and positioning adapted for pregnancy. We screen for the conditions where we'd defer to your maternity provider first, so tell us about any complications you've been advised about.

When in pregnancy can I start?

Any trimester. Many patients start when pain appears, often in the second or third trimester, though earlier is fine.

Will I be comfortable lying on the table?

Yes. We use positioning and supports designed for pregnancy, and we adapt as the pregnancy progresses.

Can I keep coming after the baby arrives?

Yes. Postpartum recovery, including the demands of carrying and feeding an infant, is a common reason patients come back.
